Quick answer

A roll-off dumpster is an open-top steel bin that a truck rolls off its bed and onto your property. You fill it over 7 to 14 days, then the truck returns and hauls it away. Sizes run 10 to 40 yards. The national average price was $586 for a 10-yard bin and $906 for a 40-yard bin in October 2026, per Dumpsters.com.

What roll off dumpster rental colorado covers

Roll-off rental covers the bin, the delivery, the pickup, and the disposal of the load. You get a set rental period, usually 7 to 14 days, and a set weight allowance. You load the bin yourself, on your own schedule.

The bin sits on your driveway, on the street with a permit, or on a jobsite. The truck uses a hydraulic bed to roll the bin off and on. The whole drop takes a few minutes once the truck is in place.

Roll-off is the standard choice for remodels, cleanouts, roofing, and construction across Colorado. It beats hauling loads to the dump yourself. One bin replaces many pickup trips.

How roll-off delivery works

  1. You pick a size and a delivery date when you book.
  2. The truck arrives and sets the bin on boards to protect the surface.
  3. You load the bin over your rental period.
  4. You call or schedule a pickup when the bin is full.
  5. The truck hauls the bin away and the waste goes to a landfill or transfer station.

The truck needs about 10.5 feet of width and 60 feet of straight clearance to set the bin down. It also needs 25 to 30 feet of vertical clearance, so watch for low wires and tree limbs.

What you cannot put in a roll-off dumpster

Landfills ban some items. Do not load hazardous waste, paint, chemicals, solvents, tires, batteries, asbestos, or liquids. Do not load refrigerators with freon unless the freon is removed first.

Electronics, mattresses, and appliances are usually fine, but rules vary by landfill. Ask at booking if you have an unusual item. A banned item in the load can draw a fine from the landfill, and that fine passes to the renter.

Keep the load level with the top rail. An overfilled bin cannot be hauled safely. The driver may refuse pickup until you level the load.

When Roll Off Dumpster Rental Is Wrong for You

A roll-off bin is wrong where the truck cannot reach. Narrow alleys, low wires, and steep drives block delivery. A smaller bin or a street spot with a permit is the better call. See the Colorado permit guide before you book a street spot.

It is wrong for a tiny load too. One room of junk does not need a big bin. A 10-yard dumpster handles it for less. A small bin also needs less room on the driveway.

Skip it for street placement without a permit. Most cities need a permit for street placement. Denver permits take about 5 business days through DOTI.

Frequently asked questions

How does a roll-off dumpster work?

A truck rolls the steel bin off its bed and onto your driveway or jobsite. You load it over 7 to 14 days. Then the truck returns, loads the bin back on, and hauls it to a landfill or transfer station.

What size roll-off dumpster do I need?

A 10-yard bin fits a small cleanout. A 20-yard bin fits a kitchen remodel or multi-room cleanout. A 30-yard bin fits a whole-house cleanout. A 40-yard bin fits demolition. Use the dumpster size calculator if you are unsure.

How much does a roll-off dumpster cost in Colorado?

Published Colorado ranges run from about $300 for a 10-yard bin to about $906 for a 40-yard bin on national average. All American Dumpster lists a 20-yard bin at $350 in Colorado Springs. Our exact price is confirmed when you book.

Do I need a permit for a roll-off dumpster in Colorado?

No permit is needed when the bin sits on your own driveway. Street placement usually needs a city permit. Denver requires a DOTI street permit with about 5 business days of lead time. Fort Collins charges $25 for a Permit to Obstruct.

How long can I keep a roll-off dumpster?

Rental terms usually run 7 to 14 days, with 7 days the most common. Extra days typically cost $10 to $25 per day. Choose your rental length at booking and extend early if the job runs long.
