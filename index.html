What can go in a dumpster

Most household and building debris is fine. Old furniture, boxes, clothes, wood, drywall, flooring, cabinets, roofing shingles, and yard waste all go in. Concrete, dirt, brick, and tile go in too, but only in a small box. Heavy material hits the weight limit fast.

Some items are never allowed. Paint, solvents, chemicals, asbestos, batteries, tires, and medical waste are hazardous. They need a county hazardous waste facility, not a dumpster. Appliances with freon need the freon removed first. Mattresses are allowed by most haulers, but some landfills charge extra for them. Do you need a permit in Colorado?

A dumpster on your own driveway needs no permit in most Colorado cities. A dumpster on a public street, sidewalk, or alley usually does. The city owns that space, and it wants to know what sits on it.

Denver requires a Right-of-Way Revocable Street Occupancy Permit from DOTI. Call 303-446-3759. Allow about five business days. The street permit fee typically runs $75 to $150. Colorado Springs requires a Land Use Review revocable permit. Call 719-385-5905. Fort Collins charges $25 for a Permit to Obstruct through the Engineering Department at 281 N College Ave. Call 970-221-6605. Pueblo routes street placement through the Transportation Division. Other cities follow the same idea: check with the city Public Works office before the delivery date. Our Colorado permit guide lists the steps city by city.

Will a dumpster damage my driveway?

A dumpster can mark a driveway. Steel rails sit on the surface, and a full box is heavy. The risk is highest on fresh asphalt, thin concrete, and pavers. It is lowest on old concrete and gravel.

Boards help a lot. We place plywood boards under the rails to spread the weight. You can also lay your own boards before the truck arrives. Keep the spot dry if you can. Wet asphalt marks more easily. Do not place a dumpster on a driveway that was poured in the last few weeks. Read the full driveway protection guide before delivery day.

Weight limits and overage fees

Every dumpster has a weight limit, not just a size. The limit is measured in tons. A 10-yard usually allows 1 to 3 tons. A 20-yard usually allows 2 to 4 tons. A 30-yard usually allows 3 to 5 tons. A 40-yard usually allows 4 to 6 tons. The exact allowance is set when you book.

Going over costs money. Most operators charge $40 to $100 per extra ton. One national lister goes to $250 per ton. The bill adds up fast on heavy loads. Concrete and dirt are the worst offenders. One cubic yard of concrete weighs about two tons. That is why heavy debris belongs in a 10-yard box. The weight limits guide shows the math by material.

Built for Colorado

Colorado is not flat, dry, or mild. A dumpster that works in Texas may not work on a steep driveway in the mountains. We plan for Colorado conditions on every delivery.

High altitude and dry air

Colorado Springs sits more than 6,000 feet above sea level. The air is thin and dry. Sun hits hard at this height. Wood dries out and cracks. Asphalt and concrete take a beating from the swings between hot days and cold nights.

Freeze-thaw winters

Winter brings freeze and thaw, over and over. Water gets into small cracks, freezes, and opens them wider. A full dumpster is heavy, and a cracked driveway marks fast. Boards under the rails spread the weight and protect the surface. Keep the spot dry if you can. Wet asphalt marks more easily.

Mountain towns and steep driveways

We deliver to mountain towns like Aspen, Vail, Telluride, Steamboat Springs, Estes Park, and Leadville. Many homes there sit on steep, narrow driveways. Winter access and snow weight

Snow and rain add weight to an open dumpster. Wet debris weighs more than dry debris. Extra weight can push a load over its limit and trigger overage fees. Cover the box when snow or rain is coming. Clear the driveway of snow before delivery day so the truck can reach the spot and place the box safely.
