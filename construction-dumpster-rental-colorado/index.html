Quick answer

A construction dumpster is a roll-off bin for building waste: wood framing, drywall, flooring, siding, and fixtures. Sizes run 10 to 40 yards, and the 30-yard bin is the common pick for a full build. Published Colorado prices run from about $330 for a 20-yard bin in Denver to about $906 for a 40-yard bin on national average.

What construction dumpster rental covers

Construction rental covers the waste a build or remodel makes. Wood framing, drywall, plywood, flooring, siding, shingles, and old fixtures all fit. The bin sits on the jobsite and the crew loads it as the work moves.

One-off remodels get a single bin for 7 to 14 days. Full builds get scheduled swaps: a full bin out, an empty bin in, on a set day each week. The site stays clear and the crew never waits on a bin.

Separate heavy debris from light waste. Concrete, dirt, and brick need their own small bin. Mixed loads hit weight limits fast and draw overage fees.

Best jobs for a construction dumpster

  • New home builds and additions
  • Full home remodels and gut jobs
  • Kitchen and bathroom remodels
  • Commercial build-outs and refits
  • Roofing tear-offs on a build
  • Deck, fence, and siding replacement
  • Site cleanup at the end of a job

For a full build, a 30-yard dumpster or 40-yard dumpster is the usual pick. For a single-room remodel, use a 20-yard dumpster.

Keeping a jobsite clear with swaps

A jobsite that waits on a bin loses a day. Set the swap schedule when you book. Weekly swaps fit most remodels. Large builds may need two swaps a week.

Place the bin where the crew can reach it all day, but where the truck can reach it too. The truck needs about 10.5 feet of width and 60 feet of straight clearance, plus 25 to 30 feet of vertical clearance.

Load the bin level with the top rail. An overfilled bin cannot be hauled, and it holds up the swap. Put heavy material low and light waste on top.

What cannot go in a construction dumpster

Landfills ban hazardous waste, paint, chemicals, solvents, asbestos, and liquids. Do not load them. Treated wood and railroad ties may also be banned at some landfills, so ask at booking.

Keep concrete and dirt in a separate small bin. Mixed loads hit weight limits fast. A load that is too heavy draws overage fees or a refused pickup.

Cover the bin with a tarp when rain is forecast. Wet drywall and wood weigh more, and a wet load costs more at the scale.

When Construction Dumpster Rental Is Wrong for You

A construction bin is wrong for household junk only. Mixed home waste does not need a jobsite bin. A residential dumpster fits that job better. You pay for jobsite service you do not need.

It is wrong for a concrete only load too. Concrete hits the weight limit in a tall bin. A concrete dumpster in a 10-yard dumpster is the right call.

Skip it for yard waste only. Branches and leaves need a clean green load. A yard waste dumpster fits that job better.

Frequently asked questions

What size dumpster do I need for construction debris?

A single-room remodel fits a 20-yard bin. A full home remodel or new build fits a 30-yard bin. Major demolition or a large build needs a 40-yard bin. Concrete, dirt, and brick always go in a 10-yard bin.

How much does a construction dumpster cost in Colorado?

Published Colorado ranges run $330 to $500 for a 20-yard bin in Denver and $345 to $635 for a 30-yard bin. The national average for a 40-yard bin was $906 in October 2026, per Dumpsters.com. Our exact price is confirmed when you book.

Can I get scheduled swaps on a construction job?

Yes. We pull the full bin and drop an empty one on the same visit, on a set day each week. Large builds can run two swaps a week. Set the swap schedule when you book.

Can I put concrete in a construction dumpster?

Only in a small bin. Concrete, dirt, and brick hit weight limits fast. Use a 10-yard dumpster for heavy debris and fill it only part way. Never fill a 30-yard or 40-yard bin with concrete.

What cannot go in a construction dumpster?

No hazardous waste, paint, chemicals, solvents, asbestos, or liquids. Treated wood and railroad ties may be banned at some landfills. Ask at booking if you have an unusual material.
