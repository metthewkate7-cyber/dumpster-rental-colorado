Dumpster Rental Cost: What You Will Pay in Colorado

Dumpster rental cost in Colorado is the first question every renter asks, and the quote is rarely the final bill. This guide breaks down every charge, shows a worked final bill, and tells you how to get an exact quote in writing.

Quick answer

A dumpster rental in Colorado usually costs $250 to $995 by size, but the final bill can run higher. Weight over the included tons, extra days, a permit, or a banned item can add hundreds. Ask for every fee in writing before you book.

What drives dumpster rental cost up or down?

Size is the first driver. A 10 yard box costs less than a 40 yard box. Weight is the second driver. Heavy debris costs more to haul and dump. Place is the third driver. Landfill fees differ by county. A box in Denver may cost more than the same box in Pueblo.

Rental days matter too. Most quotes cover 7 to 14 days. Extra days add a daily fee. Debris type matters as well. Clean concrete may go to a cheaper fill site. Mixed junk goes to a landfill at a higher tip fee.

The full fee stack: every charge that can appear on a bill

Think of your bill as a stack. Each layer adds to the total. The base rate is the biggest layer. It covers the box, delivery, pickup, and disposal up to a set weight. The rental period is the next layer. Most base rates cover 7 days. Some cover 10 or 14 days.

A worked example: how a $350 quote can become $647

Here is a real world pattern from customer reports. A renter books a 20 yard box for $350. The quote covers 7 days and 2 tons. The job runs long. The renter keeps the box 4 extra days at $15 per day. That adds $60.

The load weighs 4.2 tons at the scale. That is 2.2 tons over the limit. At $75 per ton, the overage adds $165. A mattress in the load adds a $72 surcharge in this example. The new total is $647. The quote was honest. The extras were not in the quote.

This is the most common bill shock in the niche. One Reddit user in r/Construction paid $950 in weight fees on a $250 quoted rental. The total reached $1,200 for a 30 yard box. The fix is simple. Ask for every fee in writing before the truck leaves the yard.

Why prices vary so much between Colorado cities

Landfill tip fees are the main reason. Each county sets its own fee. Distance is the second reason. A long haul from the yard to the landfill costs more in fuel and driver time. Mountain towns cost more than Front Range cities for this reason.

Demand is the third reason. Spring and summer are busy. Prices rise when every box is out on a job. Winter is slower in most of the state. Booking two or three days ahead in peak season helps you lock a slot.

Size of the job matters as well. A 10 yard box for a garage cleanout is a short, light haul. A 40 yard box full of demolition debris is a heavy haul that needs a bigger truck and a bigger tip fee.

Hidden fees to ask about before you book

Ask these five questions on every call. What weight is included? What is the per ton overage rate? What is the extra day rate? Is delivery and pickup in the price? Are there fees for mattresses, tires, or appliances?

Also ask about fuel and admin fees. Some quotes add a fuel surcharge at the end. Some add an environmental fee. A flat rate quote should state that tax, delivery, pickup, and disposal are all in the number. If a quote seems far below the market range, ask what it leaves out. A $199 sticker price rarely covers weight, tax, and both trips.

How to get an exact quote in Colorado

Have four facts ready. Your city and ZIP code. The size you need, or the job you are doing. The debris type. How many days you need the box. With those four facts, a hauler can give a firm total.

Ask for the quote in writing. It should list the base price, included tons, overage rate, extra day rate, and rental days. It should also state whether a permit is needed for your spot. Keep the written quote. If the final bill differs, the written quote is your proof.

Frequently asked questions

Why is my final bill higher than my quote?

A quote often covers the base rate only. Weight over the included tons, extra days, a banned item, a permit, or a failed trip can add to the bill. Ask for each charge in writing before you book.

What is a flat rate dumpster rental?

A flat rate bundles delivery, pickup, disposal, and a set rental period into one price. Weight over the included tons is still billed extra in most flat rate deals. Read the included tonnage before you agree.

How much does a 20 yard dumpster cost in Colorado?

Published market ranges put a 20 yard box near $400 to $560 in Colorado for a standard rental. Denver runs $330 to $500. Colorado Springs runs about $400. Your exact price depends on your city, debris, weight, and rental days.

Do I pay for delivery and pickup separately?

It depends on the quote. Some quotes bundle both trips. Others list a delivery fee apart from the base price. Ask whether delivery, pickup, tax, and disposal are all in the number you were given.

Can I get an exact price before I book?

Yes. Share your city, size, debris type, and rental days, and ask for the total in writing. A firm quote should list the base price, included tons, overage rate, and extra day rate.
