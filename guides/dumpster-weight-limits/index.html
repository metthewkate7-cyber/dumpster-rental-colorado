Dumpster Weight Limits: What You Can Load

A dumpster weight limit is the included tonnage in your rental, and it is the number behind most surprise bills. This guide shows the limit by size, the weight of each material, and how overage fees work.

Quick answer

Most boxes include 1 to 6 tons by size. A 20 yard box usually includes 2 to 4 tons. Overage runs $40 to $100 per ton in most markets, up to $200. Heavy material like concrete hits the limit long before the box looks full.

Dumpster weight limit by size

Every box has two limits. One is volume. The other is weight. The weight limit is the one that causes surprise bills. Most rentals include 1 to 6 tons, based on size. The table below shows the typical range for each box.

SizeTypical included weightBest for
10 yard1 to 3 tonsHeavy debris, small cleanout
15 yard1.5 to 3 tonsMid cleanout, flooring
20 yard2 to 4 tonsKitchen remodel, multi room cleanout
30 yard3 to 5 tonsWhole house cleanout, big renovation
40 yard4 to 6 tonsDemolition, light bulky debris

These ranges come from Dumpsters.com and hauler sizing guides. Your hauler sets the exact limit for your rental. Ask for it in writing. The included tons are part of your quote, not a detail to learn at pickup.

Weight by material: what a cubic yard really weighs

Material decides weight far more than volume does. A cubic yard of household junk is light. A cubic yard of concrete is very heavy. The table below gives typical weights per cubic yard. Use it to guess your load before you book.

MaterialTypical weight per cubic yardWhat it means
Household junkAbout 250 to 350 lbLight and bulky, volume decides
Yard waste, dryAbout 300 to 500 lbLight unless wet or soil filled
DrywallAbout 500 lbWet drywall can weigh four times more
Roofing shinglesAbout 400 to 700 lbOne square is 200 to 350 lb
Wood and framingAbout 400 to 600 lbMid weight, nails add little
Dirt and soilAbout 2,000 to 2,200 lbHeavy, use a small box
ConcreteAbout 4,000 lbVery heavy, 10 yard box only
BrickAbout 3,000 lbVery heavy, 10 yard box only

Weights are typical industry figures, not exact for any one load. Wet material weighs more. Plaster weighs about three times drywall per square foot. When your debris is heavy, pick the small box and load it level.

How overage fees work

Overage is the fee for weight above your included tons. The most common rate is $40 to $100 per ton. Some markets charge up to $200 per ton. Colorado operators publish $65 to $85 per ton in Colorado Springs and $70 to $75 per ton in Denver. Your rate is set by your hauler and your city.

The math is simple. A 20 yard box with 2 tons included comes back at 4.2 tons. That is 2.2 tons over. At $75 per ton, the fee is $165. One Reddit user in r/PeoriaIL saw a bill rise from $439 to over $1,000 after a 20 yard box came in 6,080 pounds over the limit. That is about 3 tons over at a high per ton rate.

Overage fees can take weeks to appear. Landfill and driver reports can take up to 120 days to process, per Dumpsters.com. Keep your written quote and your scale ticket if you get one. They are your record if a late fee shows up.

What happens at the scale

The truck is weighed twice. First it is weighed full on the way to the landfill. Then it is weighed empty after dumping. The gap is your load weight. The landfill scale is the official number. Your guess at home does not count.

An overweight load can also be a legal problem. Trucks have a legal road weight. A load over that limit cannot be hauled. The driver must refuse it. That leaves the box on your drive until you remove material. Removal is your work and your cost.

Photograph the empty box at delivery. If debris is already inside it, photo proof shows it was not yours. Dumpsters.com advises this step in its FAQ. It takes one minute and can save a dispute over weight you did not load.

How to stay under your weight limit

First, pick the size by weight, not by volume. Heavy debris means a 10 yard box. Light debris means a bigger box is fine. Second, break up heavy items. Concrete slabs and tile break into smaller loads that pack tighter and weigh the same, but they let you see the fill line clearly.

Third, keep rain out. Cover the box with a tarp when rain or snow is forecast. Wet debris adds tons. Fourth, load heavy items first and flat. They sit low and keep the load stable. Fifth, weigh your guess. Add up the heavy items before you load. If the sum is near your limit, stop and call for a swap instead of piling on more.

Weigh your load at home before you book

You can guess your load weight with simple math. Count the heavy items first. A sofa weighs about 150 to 250 pounds. A fridge weighs about 200 to 300 pounds. A washer weighs about 150 to 200 pounds. A mattress weighs about 50 to 100 pounds.

Then count the building debris. A room of drywall weighs about 500 pounds per cubic yard. A room of wood framing weighs about 400 to 600 pounds per cubic yard. A roof square of shingles weighs 200 to 350 pounds. Add the numbers up. If the sum nears your included tons, size up or split the load.

When in doubt, ask the hauler. Tell them the job and the debris list. A good hauler will warn you before the load breaks the limit. That warning is worth more than any guess at home.

Frequently asked questions

How do I know if my dumpster is overweight?

You will know at the scale. The truck is weighed full, then weighed empty after dumping. The gap between the two weights is your load weight. If it tops your included tons, overage fees apply.

What is the weight limit for a 20 yard dumpster?

Most 20 yard boxes include 2 to 4 tons. The exact limit depends on the hauler and the market. Confirm the included tons in writing before you book.

Can I put concrete in a 20 yard dumpster?

No, not a full load. Concrete is far too heavy for a 20 yard box. Use a 10 yard box for concrete, dirt, brick, or tile, and fill it only to the fill line.

Does rain add weight to my dumpster?

Yes. Wet drywall can weigh about four times its dry weight. Rain soaked debris adds real tons. Cover the box with a tarp when rain is forecast.

What happens if my dumpster is too heavy to haul?

The driver may refuse the pickup. You must remove material until the load is under the legal limit. Some haulers charge a trip fee for the failed pickup on top of the removal work.
