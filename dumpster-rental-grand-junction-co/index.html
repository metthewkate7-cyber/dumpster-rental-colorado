Grand Junction at a glance

Grand Junction has 65,560 people at the 2020 census, and it is the county seat of Mesa County. The main ZIP codes are 81501, 81503, 81504, 81505, 81506, and 81507. Neighborhoods include the Redlands, Orchard Mesa, Lincoln Park, downtown Grand Junction, Fruitvale, and Bookcliff. The city sits where the Gunnison River joins the Colorado River, beside Colorado National Monument. It is the only top-20 Colorado city off the Front Range.

Grand Junction has about 34,867 housing units, and the median home value sits near $338,917. The Redlands holds larger homes with wide driveways and room for a 30-yard. Orchard Mesa and downtown hold older, smaller homes where a 10-yard or 15-yard fits best. Many homes sit on larger lots than Front Range homes, so driveway space is rarely a problem. Tell us about gravel drives or steep grades when you book.

A dumpster on your own driveway usually needs no permit in Grand Junction. A dumpster on the street, sidewalk, or other public right of way needs a city permit. The City of Grand Junction Community Development Department handles permits, and building permits come from Mesa County. Check with the city before you book a street placement, and allow extra days for approval. We do not list a fee here because no fixed fee was verified.

Most mixed debris from Grand Junction goes to Mesa County Landfill at 3071 US-50. The county charges a $10 minimum covering up to 380 pounds, then $32 per ton above that for loose loads. The county hazardous waste facility sits at the same address. Do not put paint, chemicals, batteries, tires, or electronics in the dumpster. Colorado bans electronics from landfills. Take those to the county hazardous waste facility instead.

Do I need a permit for a dumpster in Grand Junction?

No permit is usually needed when the dumpster sits on your own driveway. Street or sidewalk placement needs a city permit. The City of Grand Junction Community Development Department handles permits. Check with the city before booking a street spot.

Where does Grand Junction dumpster debris go?

Most mixed loads from Grand Junction go to Mesa County Landfill at 3071 US-50. The landfill charges a $10 minimum up to 380 pounds, then $32 per ton. Paint, chemicals, batteries, tires, and electronics cannot go in the dumpster, and Mesa County runs a household hazardous waste facility for those items.

What size dumpster fits an orchard cleanout in Grand Junction?

A 30-yard fits most orchard cleanouts. Orchard wood is bulky but light, so a tall dumpster works well. Keep dirt and rock out of the load, because they add weight fast. A 20-yard fits a small yard cleanout.

Can a dumpster handle Grand Junction concrete and dirt?

Yes, but only in a small dumpster. Concrete, dirt, brick, and tile are heavy, so a 10-yard is the right pick. Never load heavy debris in a 30-yard or 40-yard. Weight limits hit first, and overage runs $40-$100 per ton.
