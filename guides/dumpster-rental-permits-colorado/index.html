Do I Need a Permit for a Dumpster in Colorado?

Do I need a permit for a dumpster is a question with a simple rule and a costly exception. A box on your driveway usually needs no permit. A box on the street almost always does.

Quick answer

In most Colorado cities, a driveway box needs no permit and a street box does. Denver needs a DOTI permit, about $100 and 5 business days. Colorado Springs uses a Land Use Review permit. Fort Collins charges $25 for a Permit to Obstruct.

Do I need a permit for a dumpster?

The short answer is: it depends on where the box sits. A box fully on your own driveway or private land needs no permit in most Colorado cities. A box on a street, sidewalk, alley, or any public right of way almost always needs one.

The rule is simple to remember. Private land is yours. Public land is the city's. If any part of the box, or the truck path to it, touches public land, ask your city first. A few cities ask for a permit even on private land. That is rare, but it happens. When in doubt, call your city public works office.

Driveway vs street: the rule in plain words

Driveway placement is the easy case. The box sits on your concrete or asphalt. No permit is needed in most cities. You still need room for the truck. The truck needs about 10.5 feet of width and 60 feet of straight clearance.

Street placement is the hard case. The box blocks a lane or a parking strip. The city needs to know. It checks emergency access, traffic flow, and safety gear. Most cities ask for reflective tape, cones, or barriers around a street box. Some ask for setback space so fire trucks can pass.

Sidewalk and alley placement count as street placement. So does a box that hangs over the curb line. If the box is half on the drive and half on the street, treat it as a street box and get the permit.

How to apply, step by step

First, decide where the box will sit. Mark the spot. Measure the street width if it will sit on the road. Second, call the permit office for your city. Ask the fee, the lead time, and who can apply. Third, file the application. Some cities take it online. Some want a visit to the office.

Fourth, wait for approval. Do not book delivery before the permit is in hand. Fifth, place the box where the permit says. Keep the permit paper on site. Sixth, add safety gear if the city asks for it. Cones, reflective tape, and barriers are common asks.

Start at least two weeks before delivery day. Angi advises this lead time in its rental guide. Denver needs about 5 business days alone. A slow week can push that longer.

HOA rules: the permit nobody warns you about

A city permit is not the only approval. Your HOA may have its own rules. Some HOAs limit a box to a set number of days. Some ban street placement outright. Some want the box covered with a tarp at night. Some want advance notice in writing.

Gated communities add one more step. The driver needs a gate code. Share it with the dispatcher before delivery day. A locked gate on delivery day can mean a failed trip and a trip fee of $50 to $150.

Read your HOA rules before you book. Share them with your hauler. A short call to your HOA board can save a fine and a forced early pickup.

What a permit costs if you skip it

Skipping a street permit is a false saving. Fines run $250 to $1,000 or more in many cities. The city can order the box removed at once. It can also tow it. You still pay the rental and the removal cost.

The permit itself costs $25 to $200 in most cities. That is a small share of any rental. Get the permit, keep the paper, and place the box where it is approved. It is the cheapest part of a street rental.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a permit if the dumpster sits on my driveway?

In most Colorado cities, no. A box fully on your own driveway or private land needs no street permit. A few cities may still ask for one, so check with your city if you are unsure.

How long does a Denver street permit take?

Denver DOTI asks for about 5 business days for a Right-of-Way Revocable Street Occupancy Permit. Apply early. Only the hauling company can pull this permit in Denver.

What happens if I skip a street permit?

Fines run $250 to $1,000 or more in many cities. The city can also order the box removed or tow it. The fine costs far more than the permit fee.

Does my HOA need to approve the dumpster?

Many HOAs do. Some limit how many days a box can sit. Some ban street placement outright. Some want advance notice. Share your HOA rules with your hauler before delivery day.

Who applies for the permit, me or the hauler?

It depends on the city. In Denver, only the company can pull the street permit. In other cities, either side can apply. Ask your hauler who handles it when you book.
