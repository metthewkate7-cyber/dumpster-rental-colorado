Broomfield at a glance

Broomfield has 74,112 people at the 2020 census. It is a consolidated city and county, formed in 2001, and it is Colorado's smallest county by area. The main ZIP codes are 80020, 80021, and 80023. Neighborhoods include Interlocken, Broadlands, Anthem Highlands, Wildgrass, McKay Landing, Brandywine, Westlake Village, and Original Broomfield. The city sits between Denver and Boulder on US-36 and US-287.

Broomfield has about 32,768 housing units, and the median home value sits near $686,200. Broadlands, Anthem Highlands, Wildgrass, and McKay Landing are HOA communities, and many newer areas sit inside metro districts that add to the tax bill. Newer homes have wide driveways that fit a 30-yard easily. Original Broomfield holds older homes on smaller lots, so a 10-yard or 15-yard often fits best there.

A dumpster on your own driveway usually needs no permit in Broomfield. A dumpster on the street, sidewalk, or other public right of way needs a city permit. Check with Broomfield Public Works before you book a street placement, and allow extra days for approval. Permit fees and lead times change, so confirm the current rules with the city. We do not list a fee here because the city does not publish a fixed one for this permit.

Most mixed debris from Broomfield goes to the Denver Arapahoe Disposal Site (DADS) at 3500 S Gun Club Road in Aurora, CO 80018. The Front Range Landfill in Erie is the other option for north-side loads. Do not put paint, chemicals, batteries, tires, or electronics in the dumpster. Colorado bans electronics from landfills. Take those to a county hazardous waste site instead.
