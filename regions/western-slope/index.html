What the Western Slope is

The Western Slope is the half of Colorado west of the Continental Divide. Grand Junction is the largest city. Durango sits in the southwest. Montrose, Delta, and Fruita fill the Grand Valley. Aspen, Vail, Steamboat Springs, Telluride, and Crested Butte sit high in the mountains.

The job mix is distinct. Ski town remodels run year round. Second homes turn over between seasons. Ranch and orchard work fills boxes in the valleys. Oil and gas work near Rifle adds construction debris. Tourism drives a steady stream of renovation work.

Distance and grade shape every job. Mountain passes close in winter storms. Steep drives limit box size. A haul from Telluride or Crested Butte to the nearest landfill is long. Book early and plan for weather.

Prices on the Western Slope

Western Slope prices run higher than Front Range prices. The haul is longer and the passes are steeper. No single published range covers the whole region, so each city page lists its own market range. Grand Junction, as the largest city, has the most competitive prices in the region.

Mountain towns cost the most. Aspen, Vail, Telluride, and Crested Butte pay a premium for distance, grade, and seasonal demand. A 20 yard box in a ski town can cost well above the Front Range range for the same box. Winter access can add delay as well as cost.

Permits and disposal on the slope

The universal rule applies across the Western Slope. Driveway placement needs no permit in most cases. Street placement needs a permit from that town's Public Works office. Check with the town before a street placement. Fees and lead times differ by town.

Mountain towns add their own rules. Some resort towns limit street placement during peak season. Some limit box days on a drive. Some want the box screened from the street. Ask the town and your HOA before you book.

Disposal runs through county landfills and transfer stations. Grand Junction has the largest facility in the region. Smaller towns use county transfer stations. Ask your hauler which site takes your load. Heavy debris may go to a separate fill site.

Which size fits Western Slope jobs

The 10 and 20 yard boxes lead the slope. A 10 yard box fits a condo remodel, a cabin cleanout, and heavy debris. A 20 yard box fits a full home remodel or a multi room cleanout. Steep drives and narrow roads often rule out a 30 or 40 yard box.

Ski town remodels produce bulky, light debris. A 20 yard box suits most of them. Ranch and construction jobs in the valleys can use a 30 yard box. Demolition needs a 40 yard box, but only where the road and the drive can take the truck.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a permit for a dumpster in Aspen or Vail?

Only for street placement in most cases. A street box needs a permit from the town. Resort towns may also limit placement during peak season. Check with the town and your HOA.

Why do mountain dumpster rentals cost more?

Distance, grade, and seasonal demand. The haul to the landfill is long, the roads are steep, and winter weather slows the route. All three add cost.

What size dumpster fits a ski condo remodel?

A 10 or 20 yard box fits most condo remodels. A 10 yard box suits a single room. A 20 yard box suits a full unit remodel.

Can a dumpster reach Telluride or Crested Butte in winter?

Yes, but weather can delay delivery. Mountain passes close in storms. Book early in winter and allow a wider delivery window.

Where does Western Slope debris go?

County landfills and transfer stations take most loads. Grand Junction has the largest facility in the region. Ask your hauler which site takes your load.
