What the Central Mountains are

The Central Mountains are the high Rockies of Colorado. Leadville sits at over 10,000 feet. It is the highest incorporated city in the United States. Salida and Buena Vista sit in the Arkansas River valley below the Collegiate Peaks. Aspen, Vail, Steamboat Springs, Telluride, and Crested Butte are resort towns in the high valleys.

The job mix is seasonal. Summer brings remodels, cabin work, and construction. Winter brings snow loads, frozen pipes, and storm repairs. Second homes turn over between seasons. Short term rental owners remodel between guest stays.

Altitude and grade shape every job. Thin air slows crews. Steep drives limit box size. Snow closes passes. A delivery window in the high country is always wider than on the Front Range.

Prices in the Central Mountains

Central Mountains prices run higher than Front Range prices. The haul is long, the grade is steep, and the season is short. No single published range covers the whole region, so each town page lists its own market range.

Resort towns cost the most. Aspen, Vail, and Telluride pay a premium for distance and seasonal demand. Leadville and Salida run lower than the resort towns but higher than the Front Range. Winter access can add delay as well as cost.

Permits and disposal in the high country

The universal rule applies across the Central Mountains. Driveway placement needs no permit in most cases. Street placement needs a permit from that town's Public Works office. Check with the town before a street placement.

Resort towns add their own rules. Some limit street placement during peak season. Some limit box days on a drive. Some want the box screened from the street. Historic districts in Leadville and other old mining towns may add design rules. Ask the town and your HOA before you book.

Disposal runs through county transfer stations. High country towns have small facilities. Heavy debris may go to a separate fill site down the valley. Ask your hauler which site takes your load. Winter road closures can reroute a load to a farther site.

Which size fits mountain jobs

The 10 and 20 yard boxes lead the high country. Most cabin cleanouts fit in a 10 yard box. A small box gets up a steep, narrow mountain drive where a big box cannot go. A 10 yard box also fits a condo remodel and heavy debris. A 20 yard box fits a full home remodel or a multi room cleanout. Steep drives and narrow roads often rule out a 30 or 40 yard box.

Roofing jobs are common after snow and hail. A one layer tear-off fits a 10 yard box. A two layer tear-off needs a 20 yard box. Shingles are heavy, so weight decides before volume does.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a permit for a dumpster in Leadville?

Only for street placement in most cases. A street box needs a permit from the city. Historic districts may add their own rules. Check with the city before you book.

Why do Central Mountains rentals cost more?

Distance, grade, altitude, and seasonal demand. The haul is long, the roads are steep, and winter weather slows the route.

What size dumpster fits a cabin cleanout?

A 10 yard box fits most cabin cleanouts. Mountain drives are steep and narrow, and a small box fits where a big box cannot.

Can a dumpster reach a high mountain town in winter?

Yes, but weather can delay delivery. Snow closes passes. Book early in winter and allow a wider delivery window.

Where does Central Mountains debris go?

County transfer stations take most loads. Heavy debris may go to a separate fill site down the valley. Ask your hauler which site takes your load.
