What changes the price

Size is the biggest factor. A 40-yard bin costs more than a 10-yard bin, because it holds more and weighs more at the landfill. Rental length is next. Most rentals run 7 to 14 days, with 7 days the most common term.

Weight matters more than most people expect. Every rental includes a set tonnage. A 10-yard bin usually includes 1 to 3 tons. A 20-yard bin usually includes 2 to 4 tons. A 30-yard bin usually includes 3 to 5 tons. A 40-yard bin usually includes 4 to 6 tons.

Delivery distance matters too. A job far from the hauler yard costs more to reach. Debris type matters as well. Heavy debris like concrete costs more per ton than light household waste.

Fees to watch for

Overage is the fee that surprises people most. Go over the included weight and you pay per ton. In Colorado, overage typically runs $40 to $100 per ton, and can reach $200 per ton with some haulers. Published rates include $65 per ton at All American Dumpster, $70 per ton at Mo's Hauling, $75 per ton at HBS Trash, and $85 per ton at Dumpster Rental Dogs.

Extra days typically run $10 to $25 per day. HBS Trash charges $5 per extra day. All American Dumpster charges $25 per extra day. Dumpster Rental Dogs charges $15 per extra day in Colorado Springs. Pick the right rental length at booking to avoid the daily charge.

Delivery fees may apply on remote jobs. Dumpster Rental Dogs lists a delivery fee of about $95 in Colorado Springs. Ask at booking whether delivery is included in your quote.

An overfilled bin can draw a fee too. Keep the load level with the top rail. A banned item in the load can draw a landfill fine. Do not load hazardous waste, paint, chemicals, tires, batteries, or asbestos.
