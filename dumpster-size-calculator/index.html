Quick answer

Small cleanouts take a 10-yard box. Kitchens, multi-room cleanouts, and roofing take a 20-yard box. Whole-house cleanouts and major renovations take a 30-yard box. Full demolition takes a 40-yard box. Heavy debris like concrete always takes a 10-yard box, whatever the job.

Weight matters more than volume

Most people size a dumpster by volume. They picture how much stuff they have. Weight is the limit that bites. Every dumpster has a weight allowance in tons. Go over it and you pay overage, usually $40 to $100 per extra ton.

Heavy materials fill the weight limit fast. One cubic yard of concrete weighs about two tons. A 10-yard box of concrete can weigh close to 20 tons, far over its limit. That is why heavy debris belongs in a small box, loaded only partway. Shingles are heavy too. A roofing tear-off can weigh several tons before the box looks full. Light materials are the opposite. Furniture, boxes, and wood fill the volume first. A big box suits them fine. The weight limits guide shows the math for each material.

Common sizing mistakes

The first mistake is picking too small. People underestimate their junk. A garage that looks half full often fills a 10-yard box. A whole-house cleanout rarely fits in a 20-yard box. When in doubt, go one size up. The price gap between sizes is smaller than the cost of a second rental.

The second mistake is loading heavy debris into a big box. A 30-yard or 40-yard box full of concrete or dirt will go far over its weight limit. The overage bill can top the rental price. Heavy debris always goes in a 10-yard box.

The third mistake is filling above the rim. Debris heaped over the top edge is unsafe to haul. The driver may refuse pickup until you level the load. Fill to the top edge, no higher. If you run out of room, book a second pickup or a bigger box next time.

The fourth mistake is forgetting the driveway. A 40-yard box is 22 feet long. It needs a long, straight spot and a truck path 60 feet clear. Measure before you book. The size guide covers measuring and placement in full.

Frequently asked questions

What size dumpster do I need for a kitchen remodel?

A 20-yard dumpster fits most kitchen remodels. It holds about 6 pickup loads, or 110 to 130 bags. Cabinets, counters, flooring, and drywall all fit. If the remodel covers the whole kitchen plus an adjoining room, step up to a 30-yard.

What size dumpster do I need for a roofing tear-off?

A 20-yard dumpster fits most single-layer roofing tear-offs on an average home. Shingles are heavy, so watch the weight limit. A two-layer tear-off or a large roof may need a 20-yard plus a second load, or a bigger box with a weight check.

Can a dumpster be too big for my job?

Yes. A box that is too big costs more and takes more driveway space. A 40-yard box is also wrong for heavy debris. It will hit its weight limit while it still looks half empty. Match the box to the job, not to the biggest size on the list.

How much weight can a dumpster hold?

A 10-yard usually allows 1 to 3 tons. A 20-yard usually allows 2 to 4 tons. A 30-yard usually allows 3 to 5 tons. A 40-yard usually allows 4 to 6 tons. The exact allowance is set when you book. Overage usually costs $40 to $100 per extra ton.

What if my debris is concrete or dirt?

Pick a 10-yard dumpster. Concrete and dirt are heavy. One cubic yard of concrete weighs about two tons. A small box keeps you under the weight limit. Never load heavy debris into a 30-yard or 40-yard box.
