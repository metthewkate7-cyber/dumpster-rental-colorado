Golden at a glance

Golden has 20,399 people at the 2020 census, and it is the county seat of Jefferson County. The main ZIP codes are 80401 and 80403. Neighborhoods include downtown Golden, the Colorado School of Mines campus area, Lookout Mountain, and Genesee nearby. Golden was founded in 1859 during the gold rush, and it served as the capital of Colorado Territory from 1862 to 1867. Clear Creek runs through downtown between North and South Table Mountains.

Golden has about 8,175 housing units, and the median home value sits near $716,500. Downtown holds older homes on small lots with steep streets. The foothills hold larger homes on sloped lots where truck access needs care. Many Golden mailing addresses sit outside city limits, in unincorporated Jefferson County. The city repealed its 1 percent residential growth cap in December 2025, so new builds are picking up. Tell us about steep grades when you book.

A dumpster on your own driveway usually needs no permit in Golden. A dumpster on the street, sidewalk, or other public right of way needs a city permit. Check with Golden Public Works before you book a street placement, and allow extra days for approval. Permit fees and lead times change, so confirm the current rules with the city. We do not list a fee here because the city does not publish a fixed one for this permit.

Most mixed debris from Golden goes to the Denver Arapahoe Disposal Site (DADS) at 3500 S Gun Club Road in Aurora, CO 80018. It is the main metro landfill for Jefferson County loads. Do not put paint, chemicals, batteries, tires, or electronics in the dumpster. Colorado bans electronics from landfills. Take those to a county hazardous waste site instead.

Do I need a permit for a dumpster in Golden?

No permit is usually needed when the dumpster sits on your own driveway. Street or sidewalk placement needs a city permit from Golden Public Works. Check with the city before booking a street spot, and allow extra days for approval.

Can a dumpster truck reach a steep Golden driveway?

Often yes, but tell us the grade first. Foothills driveways can be steep, and the truck needs a safe approach. The driver may ask for a flatter spot or a street placement with a permit. Never load a dumpster on a slope where it could shift.
