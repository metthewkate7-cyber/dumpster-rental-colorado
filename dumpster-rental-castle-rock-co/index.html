Castle Rock at a glance

Castle Rock has 73,158 people at the 2020 census, and it is the county seat of Douglas County. The main ZIP codes are 80104, 80108, and 80109. Neighborhoods include downtown Castle Rock, The Meadows, Crystal Valley, Castlewood Ranch, Plum Creek, Red Hawk, Founders Village, and Park Preserve. The town is named for the castle-shaped butte above it, and it is one of the fastest growing towns in Colorado.

Castle Rock has about 28,580 housing units, and the median home value sits near $675,500. The Meadows, Founders Village, Crystal Valley, and Castle Oaks sit inside metro districts that add to the tax bill. Most homes are new, with wide driveways that fit a 30-yard easily. Downtown holds older homes on smaller lots, so a 10-yard or 15-yard often fits best there. Check your HOA and metro district rules before you book.

A dumpster on your own driveway usually needs no permit in Castle Rock. A dumpster on the street, sidewalk, or other public right of way needs a town permit. Check with Castle Rock Public Works before you book a street placement, and allow extra days for approval. Permit fees and lead times change, so confirm the current rules with the town. We do not list a fee here because the town does not publish a fixed one for this permit.

Most mixed debris from Castle Rock goes to the Denver Arapahoe Disposal Site (DADS) at 3500 S Gun Club Road in Aurora, CO 80018. It is the main metro landfill for Douglas County loads. Do not put paint, chemicals, batteries, tires, or electronics in the dumpster. Colorado bans electronics from landfills. Douglas County runs a household hazardous waste program for those items.
