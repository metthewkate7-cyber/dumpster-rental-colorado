Brighton at a glance

Brighton has 40,083 people at the 2020 census, and it is the county seat of Adams County. The main ZIP codes are 80601, 80602, and 80603. Neighborhoods include Prairie Center, Brighton Crossings, Riverdale, Bromley Park, and Todd Creek. The city sits where the South Platte River meets the Front Range suburbs, and it keeps a small-town feel while growing fast.

Brighton has about 26,991 housing units, and the median home value sits near $480,233. Prairie Center and Brighton Crossings hold newer homes with wide driveways. Older homes near downtown sit on smaller lots. The city runs no curbside trash service, so households hire a private hauler. Twice a year the city runs a Trash Bash drop-off for large items, but it refuses TVs, computers, fridges, and home improvement debris.

A dumpster on your own driveway usually needs no permit in Brighton. A dumpster on the street, sidewalk, or other public right of way needs a city permit. Check with Brighton Public Works before you book a street placement, and allow extra days for approval. Permit fees and lead times change, so confirm the current rules with the city. Do I need a permit for a dumpster in Brighton?

No permit is usually needed when the dumpster sits on your own driveway. Street or sidewalk placement needs a city permit from Brighton Public Works. Check with the city before booking a street spot, and allow extra days for approval.

Does Brighton run trash collection?

No. Brighton does not run a curbside trash service. Households hire a private hauler. The city runs a twice-yearly Trash Bash for large items, but it refuses TVs, computers, fridges, and home improvement debris. A dumpster covers those bigger jobs.

What size dumpster fits a Brighton acreage cleanout?

A 30-yard fits most acreage cleanouts. Farm buildings and sheds produce bulky light waste. Keep dirt, rock, and concrete out of a big dumpster, because they add weight fast. Heavy debris always needs a small dumpster.

Where does Brighton dumpster debris go?
