Quick answer

A demolition dumpster is a roll-off bin for tear-out waste: walls, framing, flooring, fixtures, and roofing. Light bulky debris goes in a 30-yard or 40-yard bin. Heavy debris like concrete goes in a 10-yard bin. Published Colorado prices run from about $345 for a 30-yard bin in Denver to about $906 for a 40-yard bin on national average.

What demolition dumpster rental colorado covers

Demolition rental covers the waste a tear-out makes. Torn-out walls, wood framing, drywall, flooring, cabinets, fixtures, and roofing all fit. The bin sits on the site and the crew loads it as the demo moves.

Full demolitions need the biggest bins. A 40-yard bin holds about 12 pickup loads of light bulky debris. Interior gut jobs usually fit a 30-yard bin. Small tear-outs fit a 20-yard bin.

Heavy debris needs its own bin. Concrete, dirt, brick, and tile go in a 10-yard bin, filled only part way. Mixing heavy and light waste in one big bin hits the weight limit fast.

Heavy debris: the demolition rule

Concrete is the heaviest waste on a demo site. One cubic yard of broken concrete weighs about 2 tons. A 40-yard bin full of concrete would weigh far more than any truck can haul.

Use a 10-yard bin for concrete, dirt, and brick. Fill it only part way, to about one third of the bin height. That keeps the total weight inside the 1 to 3 ton allowance.

Overage fees in Colorado typically run $40 to $100 per ton, and can reach $200 per ton with some haulers. A separate small bin for heavy loads is almost always cheaper than the overage on a mixed load.

What cannot go in a demolition dumpster

Landfills ban hazardous waste, paint, chemicals, solvents, asbestos, and liquids. Older buildings may hide asbestos in insulation, tile, and pipe wrap. Test before you tear out, and never load suspect material.

Treated wood and railroad ties may be banned at some landfills. Ask at booking if the demo includes treated lumber. Keep concrete and dirt in a separate small bin.

Put a tarp over the bin when rain is forecast. Wet debris weighs more, and a wet load costs more at the scale.

Frequently asked questions

What size dumpster do I need for demolition?

A single-room tear-out fits a 20-yard bin. An interior gut job fits a 30-yard bin. A full house demolition needs a 40-yard bin. Concrete, dirt, and brick always go in a 10-yard bin.

How much does a demolition dumpster cost in Colorado?

Published Colorado ranges run $345 to $550 for a 30-yard bin in Denver and $530 to $635 in Denver and Colorado Springs. The national average for a 40-yard bin was $906 in October 2026, per Dumpsters.com. Our exact price is confirmed when you book.

Can I put concrete in a demolition dumpster?

Only in a small bin. Concrete, dirt, and brick hit weight limits fast. Use a 10-yard dumpster for heavy debris and fill it only part way, to about one third of the bin height.

Can I get scheduled swaps on a demolition job?

Yes. A full bin goes out and an empty bin comes in on the same visit, on a set day each week. Big demo jobs can run two swaps a week. Set the schedule when you book.

What about asbestos in an old building?

Test before you tear out. Asbestos can hide in insulation, tile, and pipe wrap in older buildings. Never load suspect material in a dumpster. It needs licensed removal and a separate disposal route.
